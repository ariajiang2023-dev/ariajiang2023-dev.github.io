Hi! I'm **Xinyi (Aria) Jiang**, an M.S. student in Economics at the
University of Wisconsin–Madison, applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

I study **how to draw credible conclusions when labeled data are
limited and measurements are noisy**. I am pursuing this question in
two connected projects.

### Semi-supervised inference for dependent data

Replicating Zhang, Brown, and Cai's work on semi-supervised mean
estimation showed me how unlabeled data can sharpen an estimate. In
economic panels and time series, however, observations are dependent
and the data-generating process can shift. After a regime switch,
pseudo-labels produced by a fitted model may become systematically
wrong. My current project studies how these errors affect
semi-supervised estimates and how to keep inference reliable, using
recession nowcasting as a testbed.

### Evaluating and designing context-aware AI tools

I run controlled experiments that test whether telling a language
model the user's professional field changes how it interprets
ambiguous terms. Leading the human scoring for this project, I found
that raters often disagree and that questions differ in difficulty.
Judging whether one answer is better than another is itself a
measurement problem, and I am now exploring measurement models such as
item response theory to evaluate these responses more reliably. Ultimately,
I want to build AI tools for public services that know when to use
context and when to ask a clarifying question, and to evaluate whether
they help staff and the public get accurate answers and recognize when
an answer needs review.

### Looking ahead

I hope to bring these methods to education. Do free and paid AI tools
give students guidance of different quality, for example on financial
aid? And if they do, does better guidance change the decisions students
make? The second question is harder, because students who pay for
premium tools differ in many ways from those who don't.
